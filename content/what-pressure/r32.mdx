narrativeIntro: |
  R-32 operates at pressures slightly higher than R-410A — about 2% across the envelope. See the operating-pressure table above (computed from the CoolProp-verified saturation dataset) for the ranges; the small R-32/R-410A saturation difference carries through them. Service equipment rated for R-410A (800 PSI high-side) is equally appropriate for R-32 from a pressure-rating standpoint.

  The substantive operational difference is the A2L safety class. R-32 is mildly flammable (low burning velocity, ≤ 10 cm/s) where R-410A is non-flammable (A1). This is materially different from R-410A handling — A2L charge limits per UL 60335-2-40, refrigerant detection where the standard requires it, A2L-rated recovery cylinders, and technician training specific to A2L apply. Treating R-32 as if it were R-410A from a safety standpoint is a structural error.

faqs:
  - q: "What's the normal operating pressure of R-32 at 95°F outdoor?"
    a: "Use the operating-pressure table above (computed from the CoolProp-verified saturation dataset) for the ranges at the 95°F rating condition. Suction follows the evaporator setpoint and load while head tracks ambient and condenser condition, so a properly-charged system reads on either side of the saturation pressure — the evaporator runs colder than ambient and the condenser runs hotter."
  - q: "How does R-32 pressure compare to R-410A?"
    a: "Very similar — about 2% higher across the envelope. Compare the operating-pressure table above (computed from the CoolProp-verified saturation dataset) with the R-410A ranges: R-32 suction and discharge both sit slightly above R-410A at the same outdoor condition. Service equipment rated for R-410A is appropriate for R-32 pressure-wise. The substantive difference is the A2L safety class, not the operating pressures."
  - q: "Is R-32 flammable?"
    a: "Yes — R-32 is ASHRAE class A2L, mildly flammable with a low burning velocity (≤ 10 cm/s) and a heat of combustion below 19,000 kJ/kg. It will propagate flame in air at standard atmospheric pressure and 60°C, but the low burning velocity limits the explosion hazard. A2L equipment design, charge limits, refrigerant detection where the standard requires it, and service procedures apply per UL 60335-2-40 and ASHRAE 15. Treating R-32 like A1 R-410A is a safety-critical error."
  - q: "What gauges and recovery equipment do I need for R-32?"
    a: "R-410A-rated 800 PSI manifolds work for R-32 pressure-wise. Recovery cylinders must be rated for A2L. A2L-specific recovery machines exist; R-410A recovery machines can be used for A2L only if specifically certified by the manufacturer — check the equipment marking. Hoses must be rated for the working pressure and resistant to refrigerant permeation."
  - q: "Why is R-32 replacing R-410A?"
    a: "Two reasons. First, GWP: R-32 at 675 falls below the AIM Act 700 threshold for new residential AC equipment; R-410A at 2088 does not. Second, capacity: R-32 has higher volumetric refrigerating capacity than R-410A, allowing roughly 10–15% smaller charge for the same cooling capacity in optimized designs. R-454B (GWP 465) is a parallel A2L choice with very similar pressure characteristics."
  - q: "What lubricant does R-32 use?"
    a: "Polyolester (POE) oil — the same as R-410A. Mineral oil and alkylbenzene are not miscible with R-32 and must not be used. POE is hygroscopic; pull vacuum to 500 microns and verify it holds for 30+ minutes before charging."
