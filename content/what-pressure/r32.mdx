  - h2: "R-32 suction pressure"
    kind: prose
    body: |-
      Suction depends mainly on the indoor coil — its temperature and airflow — so
      on a properly charged R-32 system with a 38–45°F coil it stays near {low}
      psig and moves far less than the high side. Below {low} psig points to weak
      evaporator airflow, a restriction, or an undercharge; above {low} psig points
      to a high indoor load, an overcharge, or a compressor that is not pumping.
      Confirm with [superheat](/superheat-calculator/) before touching the charge.
  - h2: "High-side pressure as the outdoor temperature climbs"
    kind: prose
    body: |-
      The high side tracks the outdoor air because the condenser rejects heat
      above ambient — about {high} psig at 95°F and higher on a hotter day. A high
      side well above the chart row for your ambient points to a dirty or blocked
      condenser, an overcharge, or non-condensables. Because R-32 has a higher
      discharge temperature than R-410A, keep condenser airflow clean.
  - h2: "Standing pressure at room temperature"
    kind: standing
    mode: sat
    extraTempsF: [68, 86]
    body: |-
      With the system off and equalized, R-32 settles to its saturation pressure at
      the temperature of the coolest part of the system (indoor or outdoor), once
      it has been off long enough to equalize. The 68°F (20°C) and 86°F (30°C) rows
      are the common room-temperature checkpoints.
