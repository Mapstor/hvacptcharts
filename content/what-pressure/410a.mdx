diagnosticSteps:
  - title: "Measure outdoor ambient and indoor return-air temperatures"
    text: "Record the outdoor dry-bulb temperature near the condenser unit (not in direct sun) and the indoor return-air temperature at the air handler. R-410A residential AC is rated at 95°F outdoor / 80°F indoor dry-bulb (67°F wet-bulb) per AHRI 210/240."
    tools: ["Outdoor dry-bulb thermometer", "Indoor return-air thermometer"]
  - title: "Read low-side and high-side pressures (R-410A-rated gauges)"
    text: "Connect the manifold gauge set to the suction and discharge service ports. R-410A pressures exceed standard R-22 gauge ranges — confirm your manifold is rated 800 PSI on the high side. Let the system run 10–15 minutes under load before recording values."
    tools: ["R-410A-rated manifold gauge set (800 PSI high-side rating minimum)", "R-410A-rated hoses"]
  - title: "Compare to expected ranges and identify the deviation"
    text: "If low-side is below the table: undercharge, liquid-line restriction, or evaporator airflow issue. If low-side is above: overcharge, dirty evaporator coil, or compressor inefficiency. If high-side is above: dirty condenser, restricted condenser airflow, overcharge, or non-condensables. If high-side is below: undercharge, low ambient, or compressor wear."
  - title: "Verify with superheat and subcooling — TXV systems use subcooling primarily"
    text: "Pressure readings alone do not pin down the cause. On a TXV system, subcooling is the primary charging metric (target 8–12°F at the condenser outlet). Superheat hovers near the TXV setpoint regardless of charge, so superheat in-range does NOT confirm correct charge on a TXV system. High superheat with low subcooling indicates undercharge; the combined calculator shows the pattern with an interpretation banner."
    tools: ["Contact or clamp-on temperature probe", "Probe insulation"]

faqs:
  - q: "What's the normal operating pressure of R-410A at 95°F outdoor?"
    a: "Read the operating-pressure table above (computed from the CoolProp-verified saturation dataset) for the 95°F rating condition. Suction runs below the outdoor ambient because the evaporator runs colder than the space it cools and follows the evaporator setpoint and indoor load; discharge runs above ambient because the condenser must reject heat at a saturation temperature higher than the outdoor air, so head tracks ambient and condenser condition."
  - q: "How does R-410A pressure compare to R-22?"
    a: "R-410A operates at roughly 60-65% higher pressures than R-22 across the envelope — both suction and discharge sit well above the equivalent R-22 readings at the same ambient (see the operating-pressure table above, computed from the CoolProp-verified saturation dataset). This spread is structural and is why R-22 equipment cannot be retrofitted to R-410A without component replacement."
  - q: "What gauge rating do I need for R-410A?"
    a: "Confirm 800 PSI rating on the high-side gauge and hoses. Standard R-22 manifolds rated 500 PSI high-side will read but should not be used at the higher pressures R-410A reaches at hot ambient conditions, where head can approach the manifold's rated limit. R-410A-specific manifolds use 5/16\" SAE service hoses (vs 1/4\" SAE for R-22) — adapters exist but reduce reliability."
  - q: "Why is R-410A being phased out?"
    a: "Its Global Warming Potential of 2088 places it above the 700 GWP threshold the AIM Act set for new residential AC equipment as of January 1, 2025. Production of R-410A continues for servicing existing equipment, but new residential AC equipment now uses R-32 (GWP 675), R-454B (GWP 465), or R-452B (GWP 698). Existing R-410A systems remain serviceable indefinitely under current regulations."
  - q: "What lubricant does R-410A use?"
    a: "Polyolester (POE) oil. Mineral oil (MO) and alkylbenzene (AB) — standard for R-22 and R-12 — are not miscible with R-410A. POE is hygroscopic; pull vacuum to 500 microns and verify it holds for 30+ minutes before charging."
  - q: "Why does my high-side pressure keep climbing during operation?"
    a: "Most likely: condenser airflow problem (dirty coil, blocked fins, restricted condenser-side path, slow fan). Less common: overcharge (verify with subcooling — high subcooling + climbing discharge is the overcharge signal), non-condensables (air or moisture left in the system from inadequate evacuation), or a restricted liquid line causing the condenser to back-fill with liquid. Address airflow first; it's the most common and easiest to verify."
