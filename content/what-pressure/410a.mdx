intro:
  - >-
    You have gauges on a home air conditioner, a number on the dial, and no easy
    way to place it. R-410A reads far higher than the R-22 systems most rules of
    thumb were written for, so a perfectly healthy pressure can look alarming.
  - >-
    Guess high and you vent refrigerant chasing an overcharge that was never
    there; guess low and you add refrigerant on top of a full system. Either way
    you leave the equipment worse than you found it.
  - >-
    This page gives the low and high side to expect at each outdoor temperature,
    the standing pressure with the system off, and how R-410A lines up against
    R-32 and R-454B — every number computed from the saturation dataset.

sections:

    kind: residential-chart
    body: |-
      Find your outdoor temperature down the left column. The high side is what
      tracks the weather; the low side is the number to sanity-check against your
      indoor coil.
  - h2: "Suction pressure tracks the indoor load"
    kind: prose
    body: |-
      The low side follows the indoor coil, not the outdoor weather, so on a
      properly charged system with a 38–45°F coil it sits near {low} psig whether
      it is 75°F or 105°F outside. A low side well below {low} psig points to weak
      evaporator airflow, a restriction, or an undercharge; well above {low} psig
      points to a high indoor load, an overcharge, or a compressor that is not
      pumping. Confirm with [superheat](/superheat-calculator/) before you touch
      the charge.
  - h2: "Standing pressure (system off)"
    kind: standing
    mode: bubble
    body: |-
      With the system off and the pressures equalized, R-410A settles to the
      saturation pressure at whatever temperature the coils have reached. This is
      what you read on a cold start or before recovery.
  - h2: "Checking the charge with these pressures"
    kind: prose
    body: |-
      Pressure alone does not confirm a charge. On the TXV systems R-410A uses,
      subcooling is the primary check — measure it with the
      [subcooling calculator](/subcooling-calculator/) and use
      [superheat](/superheat-calculator/) as the cross-check. The
      [pressure diagnostic tool](/system-pressure-diagnostic-calculator/) walks a
      low-side and high-side reading to the likely cause.
  - h2: "R-410A vs R-32 vs R-454B at the same conditions"
    kind: comparison-anchor
    compareSlugs: ["r-410a", "r-32", "r-454b"]
    body: |-
      At the same 95°F condition R-32 runs a little above R-410A and R-454B a
      little below. R-410A itself is about {cmp_dew_pct} on the low side and
      {cmp_bub_pct} on the high side versus R-32. The gaps are small, but the
      three are not interchangeable in a given system.
  - h2: "Readings that point to a problem"
    kind: prose
    body: |-
      At 95°F outdoors a healthy system shows about {high} psig high side and
      {low} psig low side. A high side well above {high} psig with a normal low
      side points to a dirty or blocked condenser, an overcharge, or
      non-condensables; a low side below {low} psig with high superheat points to
      an undercharge or a liquid-line restriction; a low side above {low} psig
      with low superheat points to an overcharge or a failing compressor. Confirm
      the direction with the calculators before adding or removing refrigerant.
faqs:
  - q: "What should R-410A pressures be at 80, 90 and 100°F?"
    a: |-
      The low side stays near {low} psig at any outdoor temperature — only the
      high side moves. The high side runs about {high80} psig at 80°F, {high90}
      psig at 90°F, and {high100} psig at 100°F outdoors. Read the full chart
      above for every 5°F step.
  - q: "Is 400 psi on the high side normal for R-410A?"
    a: |-
      It depends on how hot it is outside. Around 100°F outdoors the normal
      high-side band is about {high100} psig, so 400 sits inside it; on a mild
      80°F day the band is about {high80} psig, so 400 would be high. Check your
      outdoor temperature against the chart before calling it a fault — a reading
      far above the row for your ambient is the real warning sign.
  - q: "What is R-410A standing pressure?"
    a: |-
      With the system off and equalized, R-410A settles to its saturation
      pressure at the coil temperature — about {stand75} psig near room
      temperature. The standing-pressure table above gives it from 65 to 115°F. A
      standing pressure far from the value for the ambient means the charge or the
      temperature is not what you think.
  - q: "What should R-410A suction pressure be?"
    a: |-
      About {low} psig on a properly charged system with a 38–45°F indoor coil,
      and it barely changes with the outdoor temperature because it follows the
      coil. A suction well below {low} psig usually means low evaporator airflow,
      a restriction, or an undercharge; well above usually means an overcharge or
      a compressor problem. Check [superheat](/superheat-calculator/) before
      adjusting.
