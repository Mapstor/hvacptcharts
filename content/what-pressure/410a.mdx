  - >-
    You have gauges on a home air conditioner, a number on the dial, and no easy
    way to place it. At 95°F outdoors R-410A reads {cmp_r22_low} on the low side
    and {cmp_r22_high} on the high side than R-22, so a perfectly healthy pressure
    can look alarming if you learned on R-22.
  - >-
    Guess high and you recover refrigerant chasing an overcharge that was never
    there; guess low and you add refrigerant on top of a full system. Either way
    you leave the equipment worse than you found it.
  - >-
    This page gives the low and high side to expect at each outdoor temperature,
    the standing pressure with the system off, and how R-410A lines up against
    R-32 and R-454B.

sections:

    kind: residential-chart
    body: |-
      Find your outdoor temperature down the left column. The high side is what
      tracks the weather; the low side is the number to sanity-check against your
      indoor coil.
  - h2: "Suction pressure tracks the indoor load"
    kind: prose
    body: |-
      The low side depends mainly on the indoor coil — its temperature and airflow
      — not the outdoor weather, so on a properly charged system with a 38–45°F
      coil it sits near {low} psig and moves far less than the high side. A low
      side well below {low} psig points to weak evaporator airflow, a restriction,
      or an undercharge; well above {low} psig points to a high indoor load, an
      overcharge, or a compressor that is not pumping. Confirm with
      [superheat](/superheat-calculator/) before you touch the charge.
  - h2: "Standing pressure (system off)"
    kind: standing
    mode: bubble
    body: |-
      With the system off and the pressures equalized, R-410A settles to the
      saturation pressure at the temperature of the coolest part of the system
      (indoor or outdoor), once it has been off long enough to equalize. This is
      what you read on a cold start or before recovery.
  - h2: "Checking the charge with these pressures"
    kind: prose
    body: |-
      Pressure alone does not confirm a charge. On the TXV systems R-410A uses,

      [pressure diagnostic tool](/system-pressure-diagnostic-calculator/) walks a

faqs:
  - q: "What should R-410A pressures be at 80, 90 and 100°F?"
    a: |-
      The low side depends mainly on the indoor coil, so it moves far less than the
      high side. The high side runs about {high80} psig at 80°F, {high90} psig at
      90°F, and {high100} psig at 100°F outdoors. Read the full chart above for
      every 5°F step.
  - q: "Is 400 psi on the high side normal for R-410A?"
    a: |-
      It depends on how hot it is outside. Around 100°F outdoors the normal
      high-side band is about {high100} psig, so 400 sits inside it; on a mild
      80°F day the band is about {high80} psig, so 400 would be high. Check your
      outdoor temperature against the chart before calling it a fault — a reading
      far above the row for your ambient is the real warning sign.
  - q: "What is R-410A standing pressure?"
    a: |-
      With the system off and equalized, R-410A settles to its saturation pressure
      at the temperature of the coolest part of the system (indoor or outdoor),
      once it has been off long enough to equalize — about {stand70} psig at 70°F
      and {stand75} psig at 75°F. The standing-pressure table above gives it from
      65 to 115°F.
  - q: "What should R-410A suction pressure be?"
    a: |-
      About {low} psig on a properly charged system with a 38–45°F indoor coil. It
      depends mainly on the indoor coil and moves far less than the high side. A
      suction well below {low} psig usually means low evaporator airflow, a
      restriction, or an undercharge; well above usually means an overcharge or a
      compressor problem. Check [superheat](/superheat-calculator/) before
      adjusting.
