sections:
  - h2: "CO₂ suction pressure for coolers and freezers"
    kind: co2-suction
    body: |-
      Suction is set by the evaporator coil temperature, read on the dew curve at
      the AHRI 1250-2020 walk-in points — a 25°F cooler coil and a −20°F freezer
      coil. The pressures are high, but the logic is the same as any other
      refrigerant: the box sets suction, not the weather.
  - h2: "The high side below and above the critical point"
    kind: co2-highside
    body: |-
      Below the critical temperature CO₂ condenses, so the high side is a saturation
      (condensing) pressure — the table gives it from 40 to 85°F. As the condensing
      temperature approaches the critical point the pressure rises steeply.
  - h2: "Standstill pressure"
    kind: co2-standstill
    body: |-
      With the system off, CO₂ settles to its saturation pressure at the ambient
      temperature — as long as that temperature is below the critical point. This is
      why CO₂ systems carry high standstill ratings and, on small systems, a relief
      path or a small holding unit.
faqs:
  - q: "What is normal R-744 suction pressure?"
    a: |-
      About {mt} psig at a 25°F cooler coil and {lt} psig at a −20°F freezer coil,
      read on the dew curve. That is several times an HFC's suction, which is normal
      for CO₂ — pair the reading with the box temperature before judging it.
  - q: "What is CO₂ standstill pressure at 70°F?"
    a: |-
      About {sat70} psig — with the system off at 70°F, CO₂ sits at its 70°F
      saturation pressure. Standstill climbs quickly with temperature and only
      exists below the {tcrit} critical temperature; above that the fluid is
      supercritical and there is no standstill saturation pressure.
  - q: "Why can't a PT chart give the CO₂ high side above 87.8°F?"
    a: |-
      Because {tcrit} is CO₂'s critical temperature ({crit} psig). Above it there is
      no liquid–vapor boundary, so there is no saturation pressure to look up. The
      system runs transcritical and the high-pressure control valve sets the
      gas-cooler pressure for efficiency — there is no single "normal" number to
      read from a chart.
