  - h2: "Why R-407C needs two temperatures: glide (dew for suction, bubble for the liquid line)"
    kind: prose
    body: |-
      R-407C is a zeotropic blend, so a single pressure has two saturation
      temperatures — a lower dew point (saturated vapor) and a higher bubble point
      (saturated liquid). The gap between them is the glide, several degrees across
      the working range. Use the dew curve for suction and
      [superheat](/superheat-calculator/); use the
      bubble curve for the liquid line and [subcooling](/subcooling-calculator/).
      Reading the wrong curve is the classic R-407C service error.
  - h2: "Running pressure in bar and kPa"
    kind: prose
    body: |-
      At 95°F outdoors the low side is about {low} psig, which is {low_bar}
      bar(gauge) or {low_kpa} kPa(gauge); the high side is about {high} psig, which
      is {high_bar} bar(gauge) or {high_kpa} kPa(gauge). The chart above lists psig
      with kPa for every 5°F step; multiply psig by 0.0689 for bar.
  - h2: "Standing pressure is a range, not one number"
    kind: standing
    mode: range
    body: |-
      With the system off and equalized, R-407C does not settle to one pressure —
      it sits across a dew-to-bubble range at the coil temperature. The spread is
      the glide, so treat standing pressure as a band, not a single target.
  - h2: "If the low side reads 28 psig"
