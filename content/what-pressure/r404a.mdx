sections:
  - h2: "R-404A suction pressure: walk-in freezer vs cooler"
    kind: application-suction
    body: |-
      Suction is set by the evaporator coil temperature, which is set by the box.
      The AHRI 1250-2020 walk-in rating points — a 25°F cooler coil and a −20°F
      freezer coil — give the two suction bands you check most.
  - h2: "Discharge (head) pressure by outdoor temperature"
    kind: commercial-head-chart
    body: |-
      Discharge is set by the condenser, which rejects heat above the outdoor air,
      so it climbs with the ambient — the same on a cooler or a freezer. Find your
      outdoor temperature down the left column.
  - h2: "Reading a walk-in freezer: one worked example"
    kind: prose
    body: |-
      Take a −20°F freezer coil at 95°F outdoors. Suction should read about {lt}
      psig and discharge about {high} psig. To check superheat, look up the dew
      saturation temperature at your measured suction pressure and subtract it from
      the suction-line temperature; for subcooling, subtract the liquid-line
      temperature from the bubble saturation temperature at the discharge pressure.
      If suction is well below {lt} psig with high superheat, suspect an undercharge,
      a restriction, or a frosted coil that needs a defrost.
  - h2: "Superheat checks"
    kind: prose
    body: |-
      R-404A has only a small glide, but still measure superheat on the dew curve
      and subcooling on the bubble curve. There is no single blanket target —
      follow the equipment or valve manufacturer's figure for the box and metering
      device. The [superheat calculator](/superheat-calculator/) and
      [subcooling calculator](/subcooling-calculator/) do the saturation lookup for
      you from the measured pressure.
  - h2: "Readings that point to a problem"
    kind: prose
    body: |-
      On a −20°F freezer at 95°F outdoors, suction near {lt} psig and discharge
      near {high} psig is healthy. Suction below {lt} psig with high superheat
      points to an undercharge, a restriction, or a coil needing defrost; suction
      above the band points to an overcharge, a failing compressor, or a box that
      is not pulling down. Discharge well above {high} psig points to a dirty
      condenser or an overcharge. Confirm before adding refrigerant.
faqs:

    a: |-
      About {lt} psig at the AHRI −20°F freezer coil temperature. Suction is set by
      the box, not the weather, so it holds near this band while the freezer is

  - q: "What should R-404A pressures be on a walk-in cooler?"
    a: |-
      Suction about {mt} psig at the AHRI 25°F cooler coil temperature, with
      discharge around {high} psig at 95°F outdoors. Cooler suction runs well above
      freezer suction because the box is warmer; match your reading to the
      application, not to a single number.
  - q: "What is normal R-404A discharge pressure?"
    a: |-
      About {high} psig at 95°F outdoors, rising on hotter days because the
      condensing temperature sits above the outdoor air. It is the same on a cooler
      or a freezer — discharge is set by the condenser and ambient, not the box.
      Read the discharge chart above for your outdoor temperature.
  - q: "What is R-404A running pressure at 95°F outdoors?"
    a: |-
      Discharge about {high} psig at 95°F outdoors; suction depends on the box —

      Always pair the suction reading with the application temperature before
      judging it.
